**Problem & User**

RocketShip PMs need to make fast, defensible prioritization decisions based on customer evidence and company strategy—not the loudest stakeholder voice.

Juno acts as a copilot for prioritization. It reads RocketShip strategy and customer signals, recommends and ranks backlog priorities, cites the evidence behind each recommendation, and provides defensible alternatives when a stakeholder request is not supported by the evidence.

The PM remains accountable for the final decision. Juno drafts and recommends; the PM reviews, adjusts, and approves.

**The Harness**

**01 Context**

Required: RocketShip Strategy One-Pager (M2 deliverable and primary strategic authority) + Zendesk P0/P1 tickets from the last 90 days + Slack #voice-of-customer from the last 90 days.

**02 Tools**

search_strategy(query) — READ · read_tickets(filter) — READ · draft_priority(insight_id, priority, rationale) — DRAFT · write_roadmap(insight_id, priority) — WRITE

**03 Loop**

Maximum 5 tool turns per prioritization request. On turn 5, return the best available ranking labeled "Turn limit reached" rather than silently truncating the process.

**04 Memory**

Per PM, per sprint. Juno remembers ranking rationales and PM overrides so it can explain why a priority changed.

**05 Permissions**

Read: auto · Draft: auto · Write: confirm · Send: blocked

**06 Verification**

Every verified priority must cite at least one strategy clause from the strategy document loaded in the current session.

**01 Context · Data Requirements**

Required Sources

Juno uses three approved sources:

RocketShip Strategy One-Pager — M2 deliverable and primary authority for strategic alignment.

Zendesk tickets tagged P0/P1 — last 90 days.

Slack #voice-of-customer — last 90 days.

Together, these sources allow Juno to connect strategic priorities with recent customer evidence.

**Excluded Sources**

Salesforce closed-lost notes and executive Slack DMs are excluded in V1.

These sources contain valuable information but may introduce disproportionate stakeholder influence into prioritization. Their exclusion ensures Juno's recommendations are grounded in approved strategy and customer evidence rather than organizational seniority or advocacy.

**SLA of Truth**

The Strategy One-Pager re-syncs on commit because it is maintained in Git.

Zendesk and Slack sources refresh hourly.

Sources older than 24 hours are displayed with a "May be stale" label.

If the strategy document is unavailable, Juno does not use a cached version or substitute its own judgment. It returns:

"No strategy source available."

No priorities are ranked until the required strategy source is available.

**02 Tools · System Capabilities**

Verb List

search_strategy(query) — READ
read_tickets(filter) — READ
draft_priority(insight_id, priority, rationale) — DRAFT
write_roadmap(insight_id, priority) — WRITE

Juno can retrieve evidence, develop a recommendation, explain its rationale, and—with PM confirmation—write an approved priority to the roadmap.

Deliberate Omissions

There is no notify_stakeholder() or post_to_slack() capability in V1.

Both are SEND actions. An incorrect draft can be reviewed and corrected by the PM before it affects anyone else. Broadcasting the same recommendation could create unnecessary stakeholder confusion or cause teams to act on an unapproved priority.

There is also no delete_ticket() or close_ticket() capability. These actions are destructive and unnecessary for the prioritization workflow.

**03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency**

Turn Ceiling & Escalation

Juno has a maximum of 5 tool turns per prioritization request.

If the process reaches turn 5, Juno returns the best available ranking and labels it:

"Turn limit reached."

Juno escalates to the PM after 3 consecutive failed tool calls.

It escalates immediately when retrieved strategy clauses materially contradict one another. Conflicting strategy is a decision for the PM, not something Juno should resolve independently.

**Latency & Cost Targets**

Target performance:

p95 latency: under 8 seconds from Process click to ranked draft.

Maximum cost: $0.12 per run.

At approximately 40 runs per PM per month across 12 PMs, estimated monthly model cost is approximately $58.

The turn ceiling therefore primarily protects user experience and latency rather than model cost.

**04 Memory · Data Requirements**

Memory is limited to one PM and one sprint.

Juno remembers the rationale behind rankings generated during the sprint so it can explain changes instead of silently producing a different recommendation.

Memory does not cross teams. Each team's strategy and prioritization context remain isolated.

**Expiry & Write Rules**

Model-generated rationale has a TTL of one sprint and expires at the sprint boundary.

PM corrections and overrides persist for 90 days and take precedence over stored model-generated recommendations.

When a previous PM override applies, Juno surfaces it before generating a new recommendation and explains that the PM previously chose a different priority.

Juno may still provide new evidence that could justify reconsidering the decision, but it does not silently overwrite the human decision.

**05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations**

**Side-effect class.**         **Permission.**
Read                            Auto
Draft                           Auto
Write                           Confirm
Send                            Block 

**Justification**

Read — Auto: Retrieval does not change shared product state. The primary downside of an unnecessary read is additional latency or model cost.

Draft — Auto: Juno can generate recommendations and rationales without approval because they remain within the PM review experience.

Write — Confirm: write_roadmap() changes shared product state that other team members may act upon. The PM must review the recommendation and its supporting evidence before approving the write.

Send — Blocked: In V1, Juno cannot independently communicate recommendations to stakeholders or teams. The PM controls how prioritization decisions are communicated.

This keeps Juno intentionally positioned as a copilot rather than an autonomous agent.

**06 Verification · AI Testing & Measuremen**t

**Verification Gate**

Every verified priority must cite at least one supporting clause from the Strategy One-Pager loaded during the current session.

The citation must faithfully represent the source. Juno may not invent, reconstruct, or modify a strategy clause to make a recommendation appear supported.

Customer evidence from Zendesk and Slack should also be cited when it contributes to the recommendation.

If Juno cannot find sufficient strategic support, the recommendation is labeled:

**Unverified**

with the explanation:

"No supporting strategy clause found."

**Failure Behavior**

Unverified priorities remain visible to the PM rather than being silently removed.

This allows the PM to determine whether:

the request lacks strategic support,

relevant evidence is missing,

the strategy itself needs clarification, or

the item should still be considered using human judgment.

An unverified priority may be drafted but cannot be written to the roadmap. write_roadmap() rejects the action and explains the failed verification requirement.

If more than half of the priorities in a run are unverified, Juno displays a result-level warning indicating that the strategy document may be missing, stale, incorrect, or insufficient for the requested prioritization.

**Success Measures**

Juno succeeds when it makes prioritization faster and more defensible without removing PM judgment.

**Target outcomes:**

Reduce weekly prioritization effort from 2 hours to 30 minutes.

Keep prioritization decisions reversed within one week to <10%.

Ensure ≥90% of prioritized items include at least two cited sources.

Ensure 100% of roadmap writes receive explicit PM confirmation.

Ensure 100% of verified priorities contain traceable strategic evidence.

**Out of Scope**

V1 excludes:

Autonomous prioritization decisions without PM review.

Automatic stakeholder communication.

Actions outside the defined verb list.

Destructive ticket actions.

Any capability assigned the blocked permission tier.

These are deliberate product boundaries designed to keep Juno a human-in-the-loop prioritization copilot, not an autonomous product management agent.
